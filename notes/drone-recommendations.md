[Facebook listing](https://www.facebook.com/marketplace/item/1657284185298612/)

## Listings to exclude

- **DJI Air 2S Fly More Combo Accessories and Controller — $315:** the title explicitly says it does not include the drone.
- **DJI Neo listings:** compact and inexpensive, but the original Neo has no obstacle avoidance.
- **Holy Stone HS720E — $120:** GPS and camera are present, but it has no obstacle avoidance and is much heavier than the DJI Mini models.
- **Unnamed, S80, SkyPillar, Yomi, SMS, E88, and similar $30–$100 drones:** camera quality, stabilization, GPS reliability, and replacement support are too uncertain for useful town reference photos.
- **Ronin gimbals, DJI RC controllers, camera gimbals, FPV accessory kits, batteries, and cases:** these are accessories, not complete camera drones.
- **Old Spark, Phantom, Mavic Pro, Autel X-Star, and GoPro Karma listings:** possible camera platforms, but their age creates substantial battery, app, parts, and support risk.

## Used-drone inspection checklist

Before paying, ask the seller to:

1. Open DJI Fly or the relevant flight app and show the exact drone model.
2. Connect the included controller and demonstrate a live camera feed.
3. Show the drone acquiring GPS and holding a steady hover in an open area.
4. Take a full-resolution still photo and show the original file.
5. Demonstrate that the gimbal levels correctly and that the app reports no sensor errors.
6. Show battery health or cycle counts and check for swelling.
7. Confirm the aircraft is removed from the seller's account and is not activation-locked.
8. Confirm the aircraft, controller, batteries, charger, and required cables are all included.

Obstacle avoidance is only an aid; it may not detect wires, thin branches, side obstacles, or objects in poor lighting. Maintain visual line of sight and set return-to-home altitude above nearby obstacles.

## U.S. regulatory note

Because these photos are being created specifically for a game, do not automatically assume the flight is recreational. The FAA says the purpose of the operation matters; non-recreational operations generally require Part 107 certification and drone registration.
